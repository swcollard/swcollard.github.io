November 2026 Update

What Changed Since the Last Version

The original research was written for the 2025-2026 market. These are the changes that matter most for a purchase now.

  • No federal tax credit for PHEVs. The $7,500 clean-vehicle credit ended for vehicles acquired after September 30, 2025. State and utility incentives may still apply.
  • All-new RAV4. The 6th-gen RAV4 is hybrid-only. It adds a cheaper FWD version, and the plug-in now has ~52 mi of EV range at a lower price than the old Prime.
  • Tougher IIHS criteria. The 2026 awards require a Good rating in the updated moderate-overlap test, which adds a rear-seat dummy. The RAV4, RAV4 PHEV and CR-V no longer qualify. The Forester and Crosstrek hybrids do.
  • New 3-row hybrids. The Hyundai Palisade Hybrid (2026) and Kia Telluride Hybrid (2027) both pair 329 hp with mid-30s MPG.
  • New PHEVs. Nissan's first Rogue Plug-in Hybrid has arrived, and the Mazda CX-90 PHEV is now the brand's only plug-in SUV.
  • Removed from this page. The Ford Escape Hybrid/PHEV and Lincoln Corsair ended production, with only leftover stock. The Explorer Hybrid has been fleet-only since 2024 and should not have been listed. Lexus dropped the TX 550h+ for 2027.
  • Final year. Hybrid Highlander production ends December 2026, and the 2027 Highlander is EV-only. The Grand Highlander keeps its hybrid.
  • Prices. Each card now shows the newest model year on sale (MY badge). Prices are base MSRP before destination unless the card says otherwise.

Brand Verdict
Still the widest hybrid lineup, with industry-best reliability, resale and a 10yr/150K battery warranty. The new RAV4 is better on efficiency, power and price, and it now offers FWD. The catch is safety: under IIHS's tougher 2026 criteria, the RAV4 (Acceptable) and RAV4 PHEV (Marginal) missed awards because of rear-seat protection in the updated moderate-overlap test. Skip the Highlander Hybrid unless the run-out deal is strong, since the 2027 Highlander is EV-only. Lexus (NX, RX, TX) covers Toyota's premium hybrids.

Brand Verdict
Honda still sells only the CR-V Hybrid in the US. There is no PHEV and no hybrid Passport or Pilot. The CR-V remains excellent to live with: class-leading 76.5 ft³ max cargo, 40 MPG and a top-tier interior. Its weak points are now sharper. It gets a Poor IIHS rating in the updated moderate-overlap test (rear passenger), so no 2026 award. It also tows only 1,000 lb, and the battery warranty is shorter outside CARB states.

Brand Verdict
Subaru's full hybrids still return 35 MPG, which trails Toyota and Honda. But both earn 2026 IIHS Top Safety Pick+ under the tougher criteria, and neither the RAV4 nor the CR-V does. Add symmetrical AWD, 8.7" ground clearance and 2026 Forester Hybrid price cuts of up to ~$2,000, and Subaru is the safety-first pick. For 2027, a Forester Wilderness Hybrid joins the lineup and Crosstrek pricing holds, apart from a +$1,000 Limited Hybrid.

Safety Verdict
This is no longer a tie. For 2026, IIHS requires a Good rating in the updated moderate-overlap test, which measures protection for a rear-seat passenger. Subaru's Forester Hybrid and Crosstrek Hybrid earn Top Safety Pick+. The RAV4 (Acceptable), RAV4 PHEV (Marginal) and CR-V (Poor) get no award. Other 2026 TSP+ hybrids on this page include the Hyundai Tucson, Santa Fe and Palisade, the Kia Sportage and Sorento (later builds), the Mazda CX-50, the Lexus NX and the BMW X5. If rear-seat safety matters to you, check each model's moderate-overlap result at iihs.org.
First-Timer's Guide

SUV Test Drive Checklist

Coming from coupes and sedans? Here's everything to evaluate. Tap items to check them off.

0 / 32 checked
🚗
Getting In & Out
SUVs sit higher than sedans — this changes everything about entry/exit
Step-in height: Can you get in without climbing? Is there a running board?
Hip point: Unlike a sedan where you drop down, SUVs should feel like sliding into a chair.
Door opening angle: Will doors open wide enough in tight parking spots?
Rear hatch: How heavy is the liftgate? Power open/close? Clears your garage ceiling?
🎯
Driving Feel (vs Your Sedan)
Higher center of gravity changes handling significantly
Body roll in turns: Take a highway on-ramp at speed. Is the lean manageable?
Braking feel: SUVs are 3,500-4,500 lbs. Do a panic stop from 40mph.
Steering weight: Is it too light (floaty) or too heavy? Does it track straight?
Wind noise: Taller profile catches more wind. Drive at 65+ mph.
Ride quality: Railroad tracks and potholes — bouncier or more absorbent than your sedan?
⚡
Hybrid-Specific Checks
Things unique to hybrid/PHEV powertrains
EV mode silence: Start the car — it may be completely silent. Comfortable with the quiet?
Engine on/off transitions: Floor it to force the engine on. Smooth or jarring?
Regenerative braking: Lift off the gas. Some hybrids slow aggressively. Which feels natural?
CVT/transmission drone: Try a hard merge — is the sound tolerable?
PHEV charge port: Where's the port? Will it reach your home charger?
👁
Visibility & Parking
Bigger blind spots but better forward view than sedans
Forward visibility: Commanding view, but can you see the hood edge?
Rear visibility: Many SUVs have tiny rear windows. Is the backup camera wide-angle?
Blind spots: Check over your shoulder during a lane change. Does BSM compensate?
Parallel park it: Mandatory. SUVs are 6-12" wider and 6-24" longer than your sedan.
360-degree camera: If available, this is a game-changer for sedan converts.
💺
Tech & Interior
Features you'll interact with every drive
Phone pairing: Connect wireless CarPlay/AA. How quick? Any lag?
Climate controls: Physical buttons or buried in touchscreen? Try adjusting without looking.
Driver assistance: Test adaptive cruise + lane centering on the highway.
USB ports & wireless pad: Count ports. Does your phone fit the wireless charger?
🛋
Practical & Lifestyle
Real-world stuff that determines daily satisfaction
Cargo floor height: How high must you lift groceries/luggage into the back?
Seat fold mechanism: One-touch? Fold flat? Can you fit a bike or IKEA flat-pack?
Garage fit: Measure your garage first. Compact SUVs are 180-185" long, 72-74" wide.
Rear passenger comfort: Sit in your own back seat. Check legroom, headroom, floor hump.
